What · The Method

We build every space twice.

Once in the computer — to fully vet structure, finishes, and views, and to bring complete understanding to every line item. Then once again in the real world. By the time we break ground, the home has already been walked, approved, and value-engineered. Surprises don't survive the model.

Connect

Create

Why · The Process

The model is the project.

Most builders manage projects through documents. Drawings get marked up. Spec sheets pile up in inboxes. Spreadsheets multiply. Every document is a snapshot of one moment, owned by one role — and the moment a decision changes, that document is wrong.

We don't manage the project through documents. We manage it through the model — a single, living, three-dimensional truth that every stakeholder reads from and writes to. Change the cabinet finish in the model and the price updates, the vendor knows, the sub sees the new spec, the client signs off in VR, the budget reflects it.

The model isn't a deliverable. The model is the project.

Since 2017 · Officially

Ten years of building homes the right way.

The methodology had been forming for years before we gave it a name. Long before "BIM" became a buzzword, our designers were modeling homes in three dimensions — not as a marketing flourish, but because it was the only way to actually understand what we were about to build. The 2D drawing told you what should happen. The model told you what would happen.

In 2017 we made it official. We coined Project Management Modeling — PMM — to describe the way we'd been working: a unified process where architecture, design, engineering, construction, and client decisions all converged on a single coordinated model. The deliverable wasn't the drawing set. The deliverable was the model, and the home that came out of it.

Over the past decade, PMM has shaped projects like Mallard Lane, Storm Meadow, Tiger Road, Three Peaks, and Hamilton Creek. Each was its own design problem; each was solved through the same fundamental discipline. The Trilogy clients who've lived inside their virtual homes before walking the real ones describe the experience the same way every time: they knew what they were getting before we broke ground, because they had already lived in it.

How · The Tools

From BIM Authority to Buildertrend, without the re-entry.

For a decade we ran the PMM process across four pieces of software that didn't talk to each other. Designers re-keyed selections from SketchUp into spreadsheets; project managers re-keyed those into Buildertrend; subs got the wrong version. PMM Tools is the bridge. Built in 2026 with AI assistance, it eliminates manual data entry across every handoff in our existing workflow — without forcing anyone to change the software they already use.
